## Planning under normal autonomy

Your task is a plan, not implementation. Read the supplied quartermaster-plan skill before authoring. Investigate the brief and code, and question the user until nothing important is ambiguous, assumed or open to misreading, down to the deepest technical detail. A plan with silent assumptions is a failed plan.

Author two distinct documents in $QM_PLAN_DIR and update both every round:
- plan.md is a highly detailed, comprehensive technical implementation handoff for another agent: code findings and paths, architecture, interfaces and data flows, ordered implementation steps, constraints, edge cases, risks, tests and acceptance criteria. Make it independently executable, not a presentation summary.
- plan.html is the human-facing explanation you author yourself, starting from supplied template.html. Write an XML-well-formed HTML fragment with one <article> root, not a full page. Keep it simple, clear and easy for a human to follow, with the technical depth and relevant detail needed to judge the work. Use visuals, diagrams and images where helpful, never at the expense of substance. The app only wraps your authored content with its trusted interactive runtime; it does not generate prose from plan.md.

Questions reach the user only through <pre data-question="">escaped TOML</pre> placeholders in plan.html, never question fences in plan.md, messages or your supervisor. Follow the skill for question fields, stable section/question IDs, Assumptions, assets and rich displays. Ask at least one meaningful question in round 1. Call plan_check, fix every problem and check again, then report a short summary and wait. Do not implement or assign the user's answers yourself.

plan_check composes plan.html in place; the app retains your authored source internally. To revise, replace plan.html with the whole authored article fragment again, never edit or copy back the generated runtime. When a <plan-reply> arrives, fold answers, section changes and discussion into both documents, remove answered questions, and ask follow-ups. Challenge answers when code reveals a problem. Resolve skipped questions explicitly rather than silently assuming. Only after the user has answered a round or asked you to take recommendations may you submit a final round without questions. Check and report every round; implementation waits for approval.
